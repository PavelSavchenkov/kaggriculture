# Why the frozen forest misses expensive additions

This is a post hoc diagnosis of the exposed owned and larger-addition panels. It changes no forecasts or models and reads no partial holdout_b results. The audit reconstructs every training leaf exactly and reproduces all 1,224 saved marginal forecasts from four models across 306 labeled pairs, with zero numerical difference.

The direct-cost forest has 925 training rows. Only eight have reference workforce at least fifteen: five at fifteen, two at sixteen, and one at seventeen. The largest training bill is 2,583. Minimum leaf size four averages these sparse expensive examples with cheaper ones; the largest leaf bill in any tree is 1,537.75. Therefore the raw forest average cannot exceed 1,537.75 for any input. A separately imposed necessary workforce lower bound can raise the final prediction, but it is weak on the severe examples. This is an estimator limitation, not a statement that the largest new upper bills are minimum costs.

The newly certified owned crop addition from parent `31772fa708d23252072b` has an upper-bill increment of 13,530 and a frozen marginal prediction of -4.14. Baseline and candidate enter the same leaf in 125 of 128 trees. The remaining trees contribute +0.60 and -4.74 cash to the forest mean. Both point predictions are around eleven and a half workers, whereas the current baseline/candidate references use eighteen and twenty-one. Its timing-residual alternatives predict positive increments of 23.85 and 32.92, but still miss the large operational upper increment.

Among the four owned marginal underestimates of at least 500, median unchanged-leaf fraction is 85.94%, compared with 53.13% on the other labeled pairs. Three of the four candidate inputs have every individual feature within its training range. A feature-range guard alone would miss them. Both extreme larger additions from parent `233a95b7075a2ccd40b2` also remain within every training feature range. In-range coordinates do not establish that the combination has adequate training support.

The mechanisms differ across cases. For the late two-crop addition `2a22298396122ce9ecad`, 90 of 128 leaves stay unchanged; increasing votes contribute +19.44 and decreasing votes -19.80, producing the near-zero marginal estimate. For new-land additions, timing formulas can respond to extra work where the forest saturates: the nine- and ten-crop variants of parent `a07bc326be8bb4f2beae` both receive the same forest increment of 16.81, while timing-plus-extra predicts 703.37 and 810.41 against reference increments of 754 each.

The twelve hardest pairs are retained with full feature changes, per-tree contributions, nearest training rows, and exact training-example weights. Full-panel records include unlabeled contracts in the support audit; error summaries use only available labels and retain their physical intervals. All eleven marginal errors of at least 500 in these panels have physical intervals containing zero. Stronger necessary bounds or schedules are needed to identify their minimum marginal costs; these intervals do not erase their fixed-budget estimation failures.

Decisions:

- Keep the accepted forest unchanged for its demonstrated ordinary cold-ordering scope.
- Do not rely on tree agreement, unchanged leaves, or individual feature ranges as sufficient confidence evidence.
- Preserve the timing-residual component as an extrapolating alternative for changed work, while recognizing that it does not solve the extreme cases.
- Prioritize selective difficulty handling using the operational query model and better hard-case evidence. The existing known-baseline worker anchor also raises owned-panel MAE (122.12 to 134.65 for the direct model), so it is not a general fix.
- Any new point-model fit needs deliberately broader high-workforce evidence and an untouched test. Merely adding trees cannot remove the current training-target limitation.
