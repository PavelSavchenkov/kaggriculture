### Opponent anticipation (design, Sep 25 evening; evidence in `ROTATION_LOG.md` and `KAGGLE_REPLAYS.md`)

Kaggle losses to top-50 players turn on races we do not see coming: melons (all 7 losses; the
opponent sells from hour 9 on day 10, we at hour 23), milk and wool sold at dawn after the
overnight recovery, strawberries held through a crash. The compiler's opponent model is the
trailing 3-day hourly mean: it predicts nothing for a product the opponent has not sold yet and
extrapolates dumping in a crash. Design:
1. Forecast per product and hour of the opponent's sales today (and next dawn), from its visible
   farm (ripe crops, held animal products: daily volume), a strong-player hourly prior learned
   from top-team replays by product and game phase (`q56.py`, `opp_prior.hpp`; halves melon
   forecast error), and this game's history (online update). Later inputs: price relative to
   base (strong players sell 30% less into crashes, `q55.py`), day 28-29 stockpiling.
2. Delivery hours per product chosen by the sale DP against that forecast (`OPUS_RACE_DP`, early
   candidates, retries when an early delivery is unroutable), passed to routing as deadlines;
   melons are sold on arrival (no shop demand, so waiting never helps once the opponent sells).
3. Test beds: frozen replays of top-10 and Kaggle games (real opponent timing), the mirror, and
   the Local-LB family. First result: forecast + DP delivery hours: mirror about +5k per game,
   frozen top-10 +2.9k [-1.9k, +7.7k].

## 2. The gaps in detail

### Gap 1: budget-blind plans (fixed by the probe X10; needs the proper design)

- 91% of trimmed units fall on the investment days 0, 6, 7, 9. Day 0 trims the 3rd sheep in 100%
  of games (plan ~$80 over budget; the next-dawn reserve ignores overnight fertilizer); day 6 asks
  for $3,242 of animals and land against $1,134 median cash.
- Gold level plans within the budget: every top team opens with 3 sheep and ends day 0 near $29;
  the Majkel fine-tune opens 1 cow + 3 sheep and buys cows on days 3-4 with cash we leave idle.
- Before X10, our lost games were the small-herd games (Local-LB and zoo): animals at day 15 14
  vs 16 in won games; milk, wool and egg gaps negative.
- Design: funding envelope from the compiler (cash + certain income today and tonight - wages -
  feed - inputs); constrained decoding in the network (min-loss covering knapsack over the count
  distributions, the same DP family as the group decoding); the reserve counts certain overnight
  income. Later: rank the feasible candidates with the value model (gap 5).

### Gap 2: contested sales (the main reason X10 still loses)

- X10's 23 zoo losses: the herd is equal or larger, but prices are lower in contested markets
  (wool $132 vs opponent $145 on similar volumes; milk $108 vs $116). Both sides sell in the same
  hour on ~93% of days where both sell wool or milk (hours 20-22); within an hour the order slot decides, and ours is
  fixed product order. Same-hour wool/milk: -$3.6 per unit; when we sell an hour earlier: +$12.1.
- Mirror games (X10 vs X10): 14 ties of 32; the rest decided by the wool, melon and strawberry
  races (margin correlation 0.6-0.7).
- Against Local-LB racers: melons sold at hours 17-23 for $155-172 while the opponent sells at
  hour 9 for $232-234 (-$5.3k own revenue per game).
- Causes in the compiler: opponent forecast = trailing 3-day mean (error 47-85% of volume, 140-160%
  for melons; a forecast from the opponent's visible harvest cuts melon error to 0.44-0.50, wool
  to 0.47-0.56); the sale DP prices our same-hour sale before the opponent's units; ties sell at the
  last hour; one fixed return deadline (hour 20) and lazy deposits.
- Design: (1) opponent supply forecast from its visible farm (ripe crop yield, held animal product,
  production calendar from public ages) plus a learned head for its sale timing; (2) a sale DP over
  hours and order slots with the opponent's same-hour orders in lockstep, allocating slots across
  products (the joint choice is small: at most 10 slots); (3) receipt value by hour passed to
  routing instead of one deadline; (4) the objective stays margin, but against this responsive
  opponent model.
- First simple step: slot allocation by revenue at stake (X13): zoo +8.9k [+7.7k, +10.1k], 222/224
  wins; Local-LB +0.1k [-0.0k, +0.2k] (LB agents sell at dawn, so rarely in the same hour).

### Gap 3: one-day horizon for inventory and labour

- Top teams keep the shed at 65-90 units at dawn, sell 27 units per day at hours 0-5 (ours 11,
  in the zoo 1), carry more overnight (66 vs 57 per night) and sell steady fractions; we sell out
  strawberries, eggs and wool on ~40% of days.
- Our same-day returns add 1.4 hires per mid-game day (+$165/day); 13 hires on 24-27% of days.
- Dropping those returns saves wages but loses the race against morning sellers (X5: -$1.8k); holding
  stock without a capacity plan overflows the shed (X8 -$23.6k, X11 -$13.2k).
- Design: in `OPPORTUNITY_night_inventory.md` (capacity plan across the night with a dawn
  sell-down, cross-day stock value, returns costed inside routing).

### Gap 4: route construction

- Our layouts are as compact as top teams' (animals 1.3-1.45 tiles from the shed vs 1.74; crops
  4.6-4.7 vs 4.6; same-day cohorts as compact or more), but we walk 2.1 moves per unit of the day's
  spanning tree vs 1.76, do 11.0 vs 12.0 actions per worker-day, and combine animal and crop work
  in one trip 50% vs 64% of the time.
- Design: route construction with top-player trip templates (pick up wheat, serve animals near the
  shed, continue to crops, deposit on the way back) and an objective that includes the Fibonacci
  wage of each extra hire and the value of each deposit (gap 3). Placement needs no change.

### Gap 5: no value estimate (root cause)

- BC probabilities are not values (network per-dollar trims -1.4k); fixed compiler rules fail
  exactly where the future matters (reserve, holding, returns); once gap 1 is fixed our models
  converge (Vadim vs w384b even head to head) and phase mixing adds nothing.
- Design: `VALUE_MODEL.md` (replay + on-policy + perturbed dawns, paired rollouts for decision
  validation); use it to rank budget-feasible candidates, candidates from several models
  (Vadim candidate, w384b), and later to value held stock and cash for the compiler.

## 3. Checked and not gaps

- Shop adaptation: top teams and our agents respond alike (about +6 sheep per Yarn Store, +2.5-3.3
  cows per milk shop, +19-28 carrots per carrot-shop unit). Note: shop draws share the night RNG
  with weed spawns (one draw per empty tile on both farms), so shop tools must replay the actions.
- Land (82% of top games stay at 3 quadrants), crop yields (ours higher per plant), discards,
  unsold stock, feeding and care, fertilizer per animal, net wheat, input-stock drift (+0.3k), tile
  placement.

## 4. Order of work (simple and robust first)

1. Budget-constrained decoding with the compiler's envelope (replaces trims; generalises X10).
2. Slot allocation by revenue at stake in the executor (X13; one function, no downside measured).
3. Visible-supply opponent forecast in the sale DP and the return plan (replaces the trailing mean).
4. Night-spanning capacity and sale plan with wage-aware returns and trip templates (gaps 3, 4).
5. Value model, then propose-and-evaluate across candidates and models; expert iteration.

Gates for each: Local-LB panel, zoo panel, C++ opponents, self-play; report own money, opponent
money and margin separately; discards < 1 per game.
