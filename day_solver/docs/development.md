# Development and tuning history

## What “trained” means here

There was no ML training, fitted policy, neural model, replay imitation network
or learned weight file. This is a hand-engineered search pipeline. Development
used public replay days to discover missing mechanics, diagnose failures and
measure heuristic and implementation changes. Its fixed search variants,
budgets and heuristics were tuned on development cases. No training dataset,
original route or internet access is needed to solve a new input.

Original replays were used offline to extract public v3 problems and establish
that a witness schedule exists. Those original worker routes, owners, hours and
pickups/deposits never enter a solver. Internal hints are proposals made by our
own cold constructor or coarse models during that solve. The included sample
schedule was generated by our solver; it is output documentation, not a warm start.

## Progression

1. Early work investigated Crop Dusta day schedules and built Python feasibility
   search around tile bundles, routes, resource matching and exact CP-SAT completion.
2. An audit found restricted extraction that omitted tomatoes, geese, eggs and
   coops. The contract/extractor were expanded; land purchases were also covered.
   Old cargo-origin labels had preferred local production when cargo was mixed;
   those labels were assumptions. Later checks retained fungible resources.
3. The user clarified feasibility as the objective. Arbitrary legal deposits
   were allowed, action/move minimization was removed as a requirement, and
   random nighttime weeds were excluded from exact end tiles.
4. Broader Python validation established reliable feasible-schedule discovery:
   frozen Gate9 passed every one of 1,323 previously unseen distinct days from
   60 unused Crop Dusta episodes, including 1,005 hard days.
5. The complete pipeline was ported to C++: route construction/repair,
   orchestration, coarse and exact model building, semantic retries and replay.
   C++ became the deployable path; Python remained an offline reference and checker.
6. V20 passed Gate13's 1,399 unseen inputs, including 1,055 hard days, with a
   330 ms median. It remains the strongest explicitly preserved held-out baseline.
7. V25 passed 1,338 of 1,340 unseen Gate14 inputs. Both remaining hard days also
   timed out under V20 and frozen Python, so the gap is not confined to the latest port.
8. Further optimization produced V30. It retains the broad native fallback and
   combines direct strict schedule materialization with partial neighborhood
   selection. It passed the development and slow-case cohorts described in
   [benchmarks.md](benchmarks.md). It was sealed at the user's request before a
   new unused-episode evaluation of V30.

## Held-out protocol

Freeze the solver, extractor/converter, validator, budgets and case-selection
rule before opening unused Crop Dusta episodes. Evaluate every complete eligible
day, including losses and both easy and hard days. Record extraction/conversion
failures and duplicate exclusions. Preserve every first attempt, including
UNKNOWN and invalid results. Check episode-level coverage as well as individual
days because days within an episode are correlated.

After a cohort closes, its failures may become development data. Test a proposed
fix on another unused cohort. Passing a repaired old input is useful diagnostic
evidence, not new held-out evidence. Do not change an old first-attempt report.
Do not sum different solver versions' successes into one frozen solver's coverage.

The target was Crop Dusta's scheduling feasibility under the fixed day inputs.
These tests do not establish a new leaderboard rating or a strategy improvement:
crop choices, economic decisions, workforce and required outcomes were fixed.
The packaged benchmark cases are all exposed; they cannot serve as a new
held-out evaluation for future changes.

## Accepted optimization lessons

- Native model construction and orchestration remove substantial Python overhead.
- Preserve real resource timing. Ignoring future seed purchase timing creates
  deceptively promising routes that cannot complete.
- Compact kernels, caching model structure within a solve, and avoiding full
  neighborhood sorts reduce implementation cost without removing required checks.
- Direct construction of a strict schedule from coarse decisions saved about
  16% paired whole-day time. V30's partial candidate selection saved another
  2.3% in its paired comparison.
- Broad fallback coverage matters. Faster local work is useful only if it also
  improves complete-day time while retaining strict successes.

## Rejected or unfinished ideas

- Precomputed route cargo summaries sped a component by 11.3% but did not
  materially shorten whole-day solves in the tested integration.
- Extra native CPU compilation and explicit low-level changes did not establish
  a whole-day gain beyond the retained configuration.
- Cutting all repair budgets from 150 to 15 ms made complete solves roughly
  2.7 times slower. A 130 ms cut was also worse; an extra cheap first probe hurt.
- Lower-bound candidate pruning saved constructor work but was neutral overall.
- V33 saved about 1.8% in a small paired comparison and passed local controls,
  but did not complete broad validation. It is not this package's solver.
- V34 and a further constructor profiler were built but never evaluated.
- Related-tile repairs solved one difficult input using a stored proposal from
  our solver; their cold integration did not fix the two public-input failures.
  Optional coarse-hour hints did not establish a cold-solve improvement either.

The retained research reports under `evidence/research/` preserve their historical
wording, including earlier “unselected” labels. This package's manifest and
documentation describe the later decision to ship V30. No retraining or
algorithm change took place during packaging. A typed preparation helper,
standalone build interface and benchmark adapter were added for reuse and tested.
