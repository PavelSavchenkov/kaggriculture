# Fresh confirmation of multiple sale edits

On 9,216 paired live games (64 new seeds, 12 opponent packages, both seats, three supplied courses and two shop panels), multiple-sale timing adds $41.55 average margin over the one-edit delivery rule (seed-cluster 95% interval $37.75–45.67). Win/tie utility increases 0.1411 percentage points (95% interval 0.0434–0.2713 percentage points). There are 5,816 gains, nine losses and 3,391 unchanged cases; worst loss $44. All checked physical state, production, stock, discards and faults match.

Against purchase repair, mean margin gain is $574.93 ($519.40–635.71), with no losses in this sample. Worst-decile final margin is -$29,079.85 versus -$29,494.04 for repair. These are component comparisons on fixed courses, not better composition selection.

The same 1,000 financial-turn limit covers all edits in a call. Candidate policy time is 6.717 ms per full game versus 5.759 ms for its paired repair control, including observations and the one-time compiler. Separate candidate/control batches have different contention; do not infer a speedup from their absolute times.

Three own prediction gaps are retained: +$11 in one Salem sheep game, and -$2 in both seats of one King sheep game. Six rival action hashes change; two King cases change rival cash by -$13 each. These do not alter checked farming or terminal stock. PREDICTION_AUDIT.json and NEGATIVE_VS_ONE_EDIT.json preserve all exceptions. Generic/pair/debug, full self-play and zero-budget checks pass in runs/history_course_checks_v0/MULTIPLE_CHECKS.json.

Keep one-edit timing as the simpler control. The preceding screen's two $54 losses are explained by earlier low-value tomato holds blocking a later $71 wool delay; a separate holding-exchange experiment is testing that tradeoff. This confirmation does not prove uniform dominance or an optimal sale plan.
