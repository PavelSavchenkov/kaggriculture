# Full public-field follow-up

Adding every public tile field, ordered worker positions and today's hire count leaves the first audit's match count unchanged: 372 of 1,280 rival states have at least one matching noncash public farm among the eight sources. The 1,488 matching pairs all involve parent or ahmed_v25. The other 908 states have no match.

The tile comparison includes crop/animal type, structure, age, stored yield, dry/unfed count, service flags, fertilizer availability/expiry, care bonus and lifespan state. These fields come from the legal observation API. The original count/placement/worker/land measurements reproduce exactly.

No source/current pair matches all public fields including cash. Ten pairs happen to match cash alone, all against king_rc4, whose crop/animal counts never match. This illustrates why splicing one observed scalar into another farm does not establish a forecast of that farm. Conversely, retaining the source account creates a different stress case.

Private inventories are not part of this audit. Matching public fields would still not prove that the supplied hidden inputs or future actions form a feasible continuation. These checks reject incompatible candidates; they do not construct compatible scenarios.

The audit reuses the exposed 128 seeds and five opponents from `../scenario_cash_audit_v0`. It is a diagnostic, not a new policy test or an independent test family. Exact inputs, binary identity and commands are in `PROTOCOL.json` and `COMMANDS.json`; measurements are in `SUMMARY.json` and per-opponent JSONL files.
